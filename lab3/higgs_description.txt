HIGGS

Source: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/280/higgs
OpenML version (98050 rows, used to create this sample): https://www.openml.org/d/23512
Original paper: P. Baldi, P. Sadowski, D. Whiteson, "Searching for exotic particles in high-energy physics with deep
learning", Nature Communications 5, 4308 (2014), https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms5308
License: CC BY 4.0

Monte Carlo simulations of proton collisions in a particle accelerator. In both classes the collision ends with the same
particles registered by the detector: a lepton (electron or muon), a neutrino (not registered directly, visible only
as "missing energy") and four jets (narrow sprays of particles created by quarks). The task is to distinguish
collisions where Higgs bosons were created (signal) from a background process that produces the same final particles.
This file is a random sample of 60000 rows (rows with missing values removed), values rounded to 4 significant digits.
60000 rows, 29 columns. The target variable is class.

Columns:

21 low-level features, measured by the detector (pT - transverse momentum, eta - pseudorapidity, i.e. angle relative
to the beam axis, phi - azimuthal angle around the beam axis, b-tag - how likely the jet comes from a b quark):
lepton_pT, lepton_eta, lepton_phi: lepton
missing_energy_magnitude, missing_energy_phi: missing energy (carried away by the neutrino)
jet1pt, jet1eta, jet1phi, jet1b-tag: jet 1
jet2pt, jet2eta, jet2phi, jet2b-tag: jet 2
jet3pt, jet3eta, jet3phi, jet3b-tag: jet 3
jet4pt, jet4eta, jet4phi, jet4b-tag: jet 4

7 high-level features, invariant masses computed by physicists from the low-level features:
m_jj: mass of two jets
m_jjj: mass of three jets
m_lv: mass of the lepton and the neutrino
m_jlv: mass of a jet, the lepton and the neutrino
m_bb: mass of the two jets tagged as b jets
m_wbb: mass of a W boson and two b jets
m_wwbb: mass of two W bosons and two b jets

class: whether the collision is a signal process with a Higgs boson (1) or a background process (0)

All features were normalized by the dataset authors, so their units are arbitrary.
